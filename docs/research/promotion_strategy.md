# Digital Scholarship as a Catalyst for Commercial Archaeology Promotion

While the primary function of the **AMS Digital Scholar (DS)** programme is internal training and workflow optimisation, the methodologies and tools we employ offer a powerful mechanism for outwardly promoting AMS's expertise to the wider academic and commercial archaeology sectors. By shifting from a closed, service-provider model to an open, digital-first approach, we can establish AMS as a pioneer in archaeological data science.

This paper outlines strategic avenues for leveraging digital scholarship to build institutional reputation and elevate the academic profiles of individual AMS experts.

## 1. Interoperable Data & "Golden" Archives

The academic world relies increasingly on clean, discoverable, and reusable data. By standardising our outputs beyond the traditional "grey literature" PDF, AMS can become a vital node in the academic research network.

*   **Zotero & CSL-JSON:** By curating highly structured, open-access bibliographies for major infrastructure projects or regional studies (e.g., the Shannon estuary), AMS provides immediate value to academic researchers. When our datasets are easier to use and cite, our reputation for rigour is directly amplified.
*   **Tropy & Dublin Core:** Applying rigorous metadata standards to our site photography ensures our visual archives are interoperable with national and international repositories, demonstrating our commitment to long-term preservation standards.

## 2. Open-Source Methodologies & Tooling

Commercial archaeology rarely shares its internal workflows, often viewing them as proprietary advantages. However, the software engineering world has proven that open-sourcing non-sensitive tooling builds immense industry capital.

*   **Sharing Obsidian Workflows:** By publishing our bespoke Dataview templates for automated site registers or our Python integrations for Zotero RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), we position AMS as a technical pioneer.
*   **Methodological Papers:** Publishing peer-reviewed or industry white papers detailing *how* we built these workflows establishes our consultants as thought leaders in archaeological informatics.

## 3. Public Annotation & Thought Leadership

While AMS currently utilises **Hypothesis** for private, confidential reviews of planning documents or EIARs, the platform's public layer presents a unique opportunity for engagement.

*   **Active Commentary:** AMS experts can use Hypothesis to publicly annotate new national guidelines (e.g., TII standards), major academic papers, or policy documents. This public "layer" of expert commentary demonstrates active engagement with the discipline's cutting edge and inserts AMS directly into the academic and policy conversation.

## 4. Interactive Heritage & Spatial Storytelling

Digital scholarship moves beyond flat text. The integration of our **Obsidian** Knowledge Graphs with spatial data derived from our **BIM** strategy (e.g., LiDAR scans, photogrammetry, or HBIM models) allows for rich, interactive outputs.

*   **StoryMaps & Digital Twins:** Publishing interactive digital models or "StoryMaps" of major excavations provides a highly engaging, visual demonstration of our expertise. These are highly effective for public outreach, client presentations, and academic conferences, moving beyond the traditional PowerPoint format.

## 5. Algorithmic Transparency and Ethical AI

As AI becomes more prevalent in research, demonstrating algorithmic transparency builds immense trust.

*   **The CRAV Protocol:** By publicly detailing how we use tools like **Research Rabbit** or **Perplexity**—and critically, how we verify their outputs via the **Check, Review, Approve, Verify (CRAV)** protocol—we demonstrate responsible, ethical adoption of advanced technology. This mitigates the "black box" stigma often associated with AI in heritage management.

## Building the Profile of Individual Experts

The institutional reputation of AMS is inextricably linked to the standing of its individual consultants and researchers. Digital scholarship provides the tools to cultivate these individual academic profiles without requiring a return to full-time academia.

### The "Consultant-Scholar" Model
The traditional divide between "academic" and "commercial" archaeology is narrowing. We can support our staff in becoming "Consultant-Scholars" through the following initiatives:

1.  **ORCID Integration:** Mandating and supporting the creation of ORCID iDs for all senior staff. Every public report, dataset, or methodology paper produced by AMS should be linked to the author's ORCID profile, building a persistent, verifiable academic record.
2.  **Micro-Publishing:** Encouraging staff to publish short, targeted insights or code snippets (e.g., a novel spatial analysis script or a unique chronological observation from a site) on the AMS technical blog or via academic pre-print servers, rather than waiting years for a final monograph.
3.  **Digital Portfolios (Obsidian Publish):** Supporting key experts in maintaining public-facing digital portfolios or curated sub-sets of their Obsidian Knowledge Graphs. This allows them to showcase their specific areas of expertise (e.g., medieval urbanism, archaeometallurgy) to the wider community.
4.  **Academic Networking (Research Rabbit):** Training staff to use tools like Research Rabbit not just for literature reviews, but to identify and connect with academic peers working on similar problems, fostering collaborative research opportunities and joint funding applications.

By investing in the digital profiles of our team members, AMS ensures that our collective expertise is visible, citable, and recognised at the highest levels of the profession.
