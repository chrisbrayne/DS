# The AMS Digital Scholar: Discovery-to-Delivery Workflow

This guide outlines the overarching vision for the **AMS Digital Scholar (DS)** ecosystem. It defines how our suite of tools operates as a unified "research chain," transforming raw archaeological data and obscure grey literature into high-fidelity, professional consultancy reports.

## 1. The Vision: The "Consultant-Scholar" Model
At AMS, we do not view research and consultancy as separate silos. The Digital Scholar programme empowers our staff to act as **Consultant-Scholars**—professionals who use academic-grade digital tools to provide commercial clients with superior site interpretations, robust risk assessments, and citable archaeological narratives.

## 2. The DS Research Chain
The ecosystem is envisaged as a continuous pipeline where each tool serves a specific, high-value function.

| Phase | Core Tools | Primary Function | AMS Advantage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Discovery** | Perplexity, ResearchRabbit | Mapping the landscape of known data. | Rapidly identify obscure TII reports and foundational academic papers. |
| **2. Collection** | Zotero, Tropy | Building the project-specific "Digital Library." | Centralised, team-wide access to every source and site photo with rich metadata. |
| **3. Annotation** | Hypothesis, Zotero Reader | Deep reading and peer-review of evidence. | Collaborative, real-time highlighting of planning conditions and site reports. |
| **4. Synthesis** | NotebookLM, Obsidian | Grounded AI reasoning and knowledge mapping. | Zero-hallucination synthesis of dense EIARs and linking contexts to finds. |
| **5. Production** | AMS Document Factory | Final report generation and delivery. | Automated compliance with AMS branding and linguistic standards. |

---

## 3. Tool-by-Tool Advantages

### **Discovery: The Intelligence Layer**
*   **Perplexity:** Unlike standard search, Perplexity provides cited summaries of regional archaeological contexts (e.g., "Mesolithic activity in Co. Clare").
*   **ResearchRabbit:** Visualises citation networks, ensuring we don't just find a paper, but also every subsequent study that has challenged or supported its findings.

### **Collection: The Reference & Visual Archive**
*   **Zotero:** Serves as the company's "Memory." It manages the vast volume of **grey literature** that is often lost in standard file-server structures.
*   **Tropy:** Bridges the gap between raw field photography and the formal archive. It ensures that every photo of a find or section is described with **Dublin Core** metadata before it leaves the project team.

### **Annotation: The Collaborative Layer**
*   **Hypothesis:** Allows senior consultants to "live-review" a team member's research by annotating the source material directly on the web or in PDF format. This ensures that the evidence-base for a DBA or EIAR is rigorously peer-reviewed.

### **Synthesis: The "Second Brain" & AI Engine**
*   **Google NotebookLM:** Provides a "Safe AI" environment. By grounding the AI strictly in our Zotero-managed PDFs, we can query thousands of pages of site data without the risk of fabricated information.
*   **Obsidian:** Acts as the long-term knowledge graph. It allows us to see connections between disparate sites (e.g., linking a specific pottery type in Co. Meath to a similar assemblage in Co. Kildare) that would otherwise remain hidden in separate folders.

---

## 4. Operational Guardrails: The CRAV Protocol
To ensure the integrity of the Digital Scholar ecosystem, all outputs must pass through the **Check, Review, Approve, Verify (CRAV)** protocol:
1.  **Check:** AI-generated summaries must be checked against the original sources.
2.  **Review:** Peer-review of annotations in Hypothesis.
3.  **Approve:** Management approval of the synthesis in Obsidian.
4.  **Verify:** Final verification of links and citations before report production.

## 5. Summary of the Workflow
1.  **Search** via ResearchRabbit/Perplexity to find the best evidence.
2.  **Sync** those sources to a Zotero Group Library.
3.  **Annotate** key findings collaboratively using Hypothesis.
4.  **Synthesise** the data in NotebookLM for rapid drafting.
5.  **Build** the final narrative in Obsidian for export to the AMS Document Engine.

*By adopting this chain, AMS ensures that every report we produce is not just a document, but a verified asset in our growing digital knowledge ecosystem.*
