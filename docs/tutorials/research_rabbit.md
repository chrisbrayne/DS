# Tutorial: Visualising Research Networks with Research Rabbit

Research Rabbit is a "Spotify for Papers" that revolutionises how the AMS team maps the research landscape for archaeological consultancy. This tutorial guides you through using it to discover connected site reports, academic papers, and grey literature.

## Prerequisites
*   A registered Research Rabbit account (using your `@ams-archaeology.com` email).
*   A **Zotero** account (highly recommended for seamless syncing).

---

## Step 1: Start with a "Seed" Paper
The most effective way to use Research Rabbit is to start with a "Seed" paper—a foundational source for your project.
1.  Log in to [researchrabbit.ai](https://www.researchrabbit.ai/).
2.  Click **"Add Papers"**.
3.  Search for a key publication (e.g., a major TII project report or a seminal paper on your site type).
4.  Add it to a new collection (e.g., `AMS - [Site Code]`).

## Step 2: Visualise the Network
Once you have added a seed paper, Research Rabbit will generate a visual map:
*   **Similar Work:** Shows papers with overlapping themes and citations.
*   **Early Work:** Displays the older, foundational research that your seed paper cited.
*   **Later Work:** Lists more recent publications that have cited your seed paper since it was published.

## Step 3: Interactive Discovery
*   **Click Nodes:** Click on any paper in the map to see its abstract, authors, and citation count.
*   **Explore Authors:** Click an author's name to see their entire publication network and find other relevant experts in the field.
*   **The "Rabbit Hole":** Add any interesting papers you find back into your collection. The map will dynamically update to find *more* papers similar to your new additions.

## Step 4: Sync with Zotero
Research Rabbit integrates directly with Zotero:
1.  Go to your Research Rabbit settings and link your **Zotero** account.
2.  Once linked, you can sync your Research Rabbit collections directly to a **Zotero Group Library**.
3.  This ensures that any new sources discovered by the AI are immediately available to the entire AMS project team.

## Best Practices for AMS
*   **Use Project Codes:** Name your collections using the AMS Site Code for easy identification.
*   **Collaborate:** Share your collections with other AMS team members to build a unified research landscape.
*   **Verify via CRAV:** AI-discovered papers are high-signal leads, but always perform a **Check, Review, Approve, Verify** check before citing them in a formal report.
